FAQ

Good to know

Is my photo uploaded anywhere?

No. All models run inside your browser. Only a species name is sent to GBIF and Wikipedia to fetch descriptions.

How accurate is disease detection?

The disease model scores about 95% on its benchmark of clean lab-style leaf photos. Real-world photos (clutter, lighting, other crops) are harder, so treat results as a screening aid and confirm with an expert.

Which crops are supported for disease detection?

Apple, blueberry, cherry, corn, grape, orange, peach, bell pepper, potato, raspberry, soybean, squash, strawberry and tomato.

Does it work for plants outside Europe?

Yes. BioCLIP covers 4,271 species photographed worldwide. A species absent from every model’s list cannot be recognised, and low confidence is shown instead.

Does it work offline?

After the first launch the models are cached, so identification and diagnosis work offline. Descriptions and taxonomy need a connection.

How it works

Architecture, models and limits.

Pipeline

Photo → resize and normalise → neural networks (ONNX Runtime Web, WebAssembly) → fusion and knowledge base → GBIF and Wikipedia enrichment.

Species identification

Two models run side by side: Pl@ntNet MobileNetV4 (7,806 species, Europe specialist) and BioCLIP-2.5 Mobile (4,271 species, worldwide). Probabilities are calibrated and averaged (0.4 / 0.6). Species both models rank in their top 3 are combined as independent evidence (noisy-OR), so agreement raises confidence.

Leaf disease detection

LeafDoctor is a MobileNetV2 trained on the PlantVillage dataset (38 classes over 14 crops, ~95% benchmark accuracy). Treatment guidance comes from a curated knowledge base. PlantVillage photos are taken on plain backgrounds, so field photos with clutter can be less accurate. Use close, well-lit, single-leaf photos.

Privacy

Photos stay in your browser. Only species names are sent to GBIF and Wikipedia.

Limits

AI can be wrong. Species outside the models’ lists and crops outside the 14 supported ones cannot be recognised. Never eat or medicate with a plant, or apply chemicals, based on this result alone. Consult a local agricultural extension service.
